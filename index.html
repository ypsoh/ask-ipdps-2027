Overview

ASK is a half-day workshop on agentic large-language-model (LLM) workflows for high-performance computing kernels, centered on sparse and irregular kernels. Its premise is the distinction between generation and agency. A generative system emits a candidate kernel from a prompt. An agentic system selects a data structure, compiles, executes on real hardware, reads performance counters, diagnoses the bottleneck, and revises. Early systems now demonstrate the promise of this closed loop, but methods and evaluation remain fragmented—especially for sparse and irregular workloads.

Sparse kernels make this question concrete. The decisions that govern their performance are largely invisible in the source: which representation to store the data in, how to reorder and partition it, and how much preprocessing to invest against the reuse it buys. These decisions depend on the input as much as on the code, interact with one another, and rarely admit a single best answer. That is an unusually good match for an agent that can try something, measure the result, and revise, and we expect it to produce workflow designs quite different from those developed for the dense case.

The workshop brings together the sparse and combinatorial kernel community and the agentic LLM community, which today publish in largely disjoint venues. We welcome workflow and system design, empirical studies of existing agents, benchmarks and evaluation methodology, compiler and tooling support, and experience reports. Negative results and failure analyses are explicitly in scope.

Call for Papers

Download a printable PDF of the ASK 2027 Call for Papers.

ASK 2027 invites original, unpublished work on agentic methods for high-performance computing kernels, with particular emphasis on sparse and irregular workloads. Submissions must not be under review at another archival venue while they are under consideration by ASK 2027.

Topics of interest

  • Agentic loops for kernel optimization: compile, run, profile, revise; counter- and roofline-guided search; autonomous autotuning
  • Sparsity format and algorithm selection by LLM agents; input-dependent specialization
  • Multi-agent decomposition of HPC tasks (generate, verify, tune, port)
  • Tool use and grounding: compilers, profilers, schedulers, performance models, and sparse DSLs (e.g., TACO, MLIR SparseTensor) as agent tools
  • Correctness and numerical verification of agent-written kernels
  • Agentic performance portability across CPUs, GPUs, and accelerators
  • Benchmarks, datasets, and evaluation methodology for HPC coding agents
  • Cost, energy, and human-in-the-loop trade-offs
  • Negative results and failure analyses

Dense, stencil, and FFT kernels are in scope when the contribution is the agentic method rather than the kernel itself.

Submission tracks

  • Full papers: up to 10 pages for complete research results and substantial system, methodology, benchmark, or empirical contributions.
  • Short papers: up to 4 pages for work in progress, position papers, experience reports, negative results, and emerging ideas. Accepted short papers will also be presented at the IPDPS cross-workshop poster session.

Formatting and submission requirements

  • Submissions must use the official IEEE conference proceedings templates for LaTeX or Microsoft Word.
  • Use US Letter paper (8.5 × 11 inches), 10-point type, single-spaced text, and the double-column IEEE conference layout.
  • The page limit includes the body, figures, and tables. References do not count toward the limit and may extend beyond it. Appendices must fit within the applicable page limit.
  • ASK uses single-anonymous review: author names, affiliations, and acknowledgments should appear in the submitted manuscript.
  • Submit one PDF through Linklings. The workshop-specific submission link will be posted here as soon as it is issued by IPDPS.

Review, reproducibility, and publication

Every submission will receive at least three reviews. Because agentic results can vary between runs, authors should report model and tool versions, sampling parameters, optimization budgets, hardware and software environments, correctness criteria, and the number of trials supporting reported results. Code, matrices, prompts, agent trajectories, and other artifacts are strongly encouraged. Supplementary material may be provided for reproducibility, but reviewers are not required to consider it when judging the paper.

Accepted full and short papers will appear in the IPDPS Workshops proceedings (IPDPSW), which are submitted for inclusion in the IEEE Xplore Digital Library. Final papers must follow the IEEE Computer Society conference-publishing instructions supplied after acceptance. At least one author of every accepted paper must register for IPDPS and present the work in person.
