Overview

This workshop addresses the design of agentic LLM workflows for sparse and irregular kernels in high-performance computing. Agentic code generation has produced substantial results for HPC kernels, with reported systems that write, tune, and port code at performance competitive with hand-tuned implementations.

Such progress has concentrated on dense kernels, where the optimization space is comparatively tractable. The relevant transformations are well established (tiling, unrolling, vectorization, loop ordering), the search space admits systematic enumeration, and performance is largely predictable from the source.

We argue that sparse kernels are less amenable to such treatment. The action space is substantially larger and less structured, spanning storage format selection, matrix reordering and partitioning, and the tradeoff between preprocessing cost and the reuse that preprocessing enables. Two properties are particular to this setting. Optimizations amortize only across repeated invocations, so an agent must reason about cost over a workload rather than a single execution, which requires that performance tradeoffs be evaluated explicitly rather than read off a timing run. Transformations alter data layout, so an agent must carry persistent state across iterations, which requires loop structures that accumulate and refine intermediate findings rather than restarting from the prompt. Together these suggest that sparse kernels warrant dedicated study rather than the direct transfer of methods developed for the dense case.

This workshop invites contributions across the full range of research applying agentic LLMs to sparse and irregular kernel development. Relevant work includes workflow and system design, empirical studies of existing agents, benchmarks and evaluation methodology, compiler and tooling support, and experience reports from practice. We are equally interested in successful and unsuccessful outcomes, and negative results and failure analyses are explicitly in scope.

Call for Contributions

We invite papers on topics including:

  • Agentic optimization loops: compile, run, profile, revise
  • Sparsity format and algorithm selection by LLM agents
  • Multi-agent decomposition of kernel development tasks
  • Compilers, profilers, and sparse DSLs as agent tools
  • Correctness and numerical verification of agent-written kernels
  • Agentic performance portability across CPUs, GPUs, and accelerators
  • Benchmarks, datasets, and evaluation methodology for HPC coding agents
  • Cost, energy, and human-in-the-loop trade-offs
  • Negative results and failure analyses

Dense, stencil, and FFT kernels are in scope when the contribution is the agentic method rather than the kernel itself.

Papers may be up to 10 pages in IEEE conference format, including references. Each submission receives at least three reviews. Accepted papers appear in the IPDPS workshop proceedings (IPDPSW) and are submitted to the IEEE Xplore Digital Library. At least one author must register and present in person.

Because agentic results vary between runs, submissions should report model version, sampling parameters, and the number of trials behind any reported number.
